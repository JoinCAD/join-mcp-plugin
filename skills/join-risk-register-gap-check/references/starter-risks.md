# Starter risks by theme

These are deliberately plain, general-purpose risks for teams to adapt. They were written for this skill and are not taken from any project's register. For each flagged theme, pick the three that fit the project best and don't duplicate something already on its register. You may adjust a word or two so a risk reads naturally for the project type (e.g. "occupied hospital" instead of "occupied facility"). Keep them generic: no names, addresses, costs, dates or details that would only be true for one project.

Each entry is **Name**, then a one-sentence description suitable for the risk's description field.

## Design Completeness & Changes
- **Incomplete design at pricing**: Pricing is based on drawings that are not fully developed, so scope may grow as the design is completed.
- **Late design decisions**: Open decisions on key systems, layouts or finishes could delay design completion and downstream procurement.
- **Discipline coordination conflicts**: Unresolved conflicts between architectural, structural and MEP documents may lead to RFIs, redesign and field rework.
- **Delegated design scope gaps**: Elements left to subcontractor design may be incompletely defined, creating scope and responsibility gaps.
- **Plan review driven redesign**: Comments from code review could require design revisions after the project has been priced.

## MEP & Building Systems
- **MEP capacity shortfall**: Mechanical or electrical systems may not have enough capacity for final loads, requiring upsizing.
- **Above-ceiling space congestion**: Limited space for ductwork, piping and conduit could force redesign or rework during installation.
- **Systems integration issues**: Controls, security and low-voltage systems may not integrate as intended, delaying turnover.
- **Fire protection scope growth**: Fire alarm, sprinkler or smoke control requirements may expand after authority review.
- **Commissioning failures**: Building systems may fail functional testing, delaying substantial completion.

## Permitting & Agency Approvals
- **Permit issuance delay**: Building permit approval could take longer than the schedule assumes.
- **Plan review comments add scope**: Agency review comments may add requirements that are not in the current design or budget.
- **Early or phased permits unavailable**: The jurisdiction may not issue early site or foundation permits, delaying the start of work.
- **Third-party agency approvals**: Approvals from agencies such as transportation, fire or health departments may take longer than planned.
- **Inspection availability**: Limited inspector availability could hold up work that needs sign-off before it can proceed.

## Utilities & Site Services
- **Insufficient utility capacity**: Existing utility service may not support the project's demand, requiring upgrades.
- **Utility company schedule**: The utility provider's timeline for new or upgraded service may not align with the construction schedule.
- **Unknown utility locations**: Underground utilities may not be where records show, causing conflicts during excavation.
- **Utility relocation required**: Existing utilities in the work area may need to be relocated before construction can proceed.
- **Off-site utility extensions**: Connecting to the nearest utility main may require off-site work beyond the current scope.

## Existing Conditions & Renovation
- **Concealed existing conditions**: Conditions hidden behind walls, floors or ceilings may differ from what the drawings assume.
- **Hazardous materials in existing construction**: Asbestos, lead paint or other hazardous materials may be found and require abatement.
- **Inaccurate as-built drawings**: Existing building records may be incomplete or wrong, affecting design and installation.
- **Work in an occupied facility**: Ongoing operations may limit work hours, access and noise, slowing progress.
- **Tie-ins and shutdowns**: Connections to existing systems may require shutdowns that are difficult to schedule.

## Geotechnical & Unforeseen Site Conditions
- **Poor or variable soils**: Soil conditions may be weaker or more variable than the geotechnical report indicates.
- **Groundwater and dewatering**: High groundwater may require dewatering or changes to foundations and excavation.
- **Rock excavation**: Rock encountered during excavation could increase cost and duration.
- **Contaminated soil**: Excavated soil may be contaminated and require special handling and disposal.
- **Buried obstructions**: Old foundations, debris or other buried obstructions may be found during excavation.

## Procurement & Long-Lead
- **Long-lead electrical equipment**: Switchgear, transformers or generators may have lead times that affect the critical path.
- **Mechanical equipment lead times**: Major mechanical equipment may arrive later than needed for installation.
- **Limited bidder coverage**: Few qualified bidders for a key trade could raise prices or limit options.
- **Supplier delivery delays**: Material deliveries may slip due to supplier backlog or shipping disruptions.
- **Sole-source or discontinued products**: Specified products may be sole-source or discontinued, requiring substitutions.

## Owner Scope & Program Changes
- **Program changes after design freeze**: Late changes to the space program could require redesign and rework.
- **Owner-furnished equipment decisions**: Late selection or delivery of owner-furnished equipment may affect design and installation.
- **Insufficient allowances**: Allowances in the budget may not cover the scope the owner ultimately selects.
- **User group requests**: Requests from end users during construction may add scope.
- **Changes to phasing or occupancy plans**: Owner changes to move-in or phasing plans could disrupt the construction sequence.

## Logistics & Site Access
- **Limited laydown area**: The site may lack space for material storage and staging, requiring off-site storage or just-in-time delivery.
- **Crane and hoisting constraints**: Site conditions may limit crane placement or hoisting capacity.
- **Traffic and public access**: Work near roads or public areas may require traffic control and restricted work hours.
- **Neighbor impacts**: Noise, vibration or dust could prompt complaints or restrictions from neighbors.
- **Adjacent construction**: Other projects nearby may compete for access, deliveries or staging space.

## Schedule & Phasing
- **Compressed schedule**: The overall duration may be too short for the scope, leaving little float.
- **Complex phasing**: Multiple phases may add handoffs, temporary work and sequencing risk.
- **Fixed completion date**: A hard opening or occupancy date leaves limited room to absorb delays.
- **Single critical path activity**: The schedule may depend heavily on one activity or trade with little recovery time.
- **Early package delays**: Delays to early release packages could push back the start of major work.

## Budget & Funding
- **Estimate exceeds budget**: The current estimate may exceed the available budget, requiring scope reductions.
- **Insufficient contingency**: Contingency may not be large enough for the level of design and site uncertainty.
- **Funding timing or approval**: Delays in funding approval could hold up design or construction.
- **Value engineering not realized**: Planned savings may not be achieved once value engineering options are reviewed.
- **Scope gaps between packages**: Scope missing between bid packages may surface as unbudgeted cost.

## Contract & Insurance
- **Liquidated damages exposure**: Delays could trigger liquidated damages under the contract.
- **Unfavorable risk transfer**: Contract terms may shift more risk to the builder than typical.
- **Non-standard insurance requirements**: Insurance or bonding requirements may exceed standard coverage.
- **Payment terms and retainage**: Payment timing or retainage terms could strain cash flow.
- **Subcontractor default**: A subcontractor may fail to perform, requiring replacement or supplemental work.

## Safety & Quality
- **High-risk work activities**: Work at height, in confined spaces or near energized systems increases safety exposure.
- **Rework from quality issues**: Installed work may not meet specifications, requiring rework.
- **Testing and inspection failures**: Third-party testing or special inspections may identify deficiencies that delay work.
- **Subcontractor safety performance**: A subcontractor's safety record or practices may not meet project standards.
- **Mock-up rejection**: Mock-ups may not be approved on the first submission, delaying production work.

## Weather & Environment
- **Weather delays**: Rain, snow or wind during a critical phase could delay work.
- **Cold weather protection**: Winter conditions may require temporary heat, enclosures or concrete protection.
- **Extreme heat**: High temperatures could reduce productivity and require schedule adjustments.
- **Stormwater and erosion control**: Stormwater management requirements may add cost or cause delays after storms.
- **Environmental restrictions**: Environmental permits or protected habitat may restrict work timing or methods.

## Envelope & Finishes
- **Water intrusion**: The building envelope may allow water intrusion, damaging interior work.
- **Curtain wall or storefront performance**: Glazing systems may fail performance testing or need design changes.
- **Roofing weather dependency**: Roofing work may be delayed by weather, holding up dry-in.
- **Late finish selections**: Delays in selecting finishes could affect procurement and installation.
- **Envelope mock-up approval**: Envelope mock-ups may require revisions before installation can proceed.

## Seismic & Structural
- **Added structural loads**: New equipment or design changes may add loads that require structural revisions.
- **Foundation design uncertainty**: The foundation design may change once site conditions are confirmed.
- **Steel fabrication and erection sequence**: Steel fabrication or erection sequencing may delay the structure.
- **Nonstructural seismic bracing**: Bracing for equipment, piping and ceilings may add cost and coordination.
- **Temporary shoring and bracing**: Temporary structural support may be more extensive than planned.

## Material Cost Escalation
- **Escalation before buyout**: Material prices may rise between the estimate and buyout.
- **Commodity price volatility**: Steel, copper and other commodity prices could fluctuate significantly.
- **Expiring price holds**: Supplier price holds may expire before contracts are signed.
- **Freight and fuel surcharges**: Transportation costs may add surcharges to material deliveries.
- **Escalation on a long duration**: A long project duration increases exposure to price increases on later work.

## Labor & Workforce
- **Skilled labor shortage**: Limited availability of skilled trades could slow progress or raise costs.
- **Subcontractor capacity**: Key subcontractors may be stretched across multiple projects.
- **Labor rate increases**: Wage increases or prevailing wage requirements could raise labor costs.
- **Workforce participation goals**: Local or diverse workforce goals may be difficult to meet.
- **Key staff turnover**: Loss of key project staff could disrupt continuity.

## Tariffs & Trade Policy
- **Tariffs on imported materials**: New or increased tariffs could raise the cost of imported materials.
- **Tariff price adjustments**: Suppliers or subcontractors may seek price adjustments due to tariffs.
- **Imported equipment duties**: Equipment manufactured overseas may be subject to new duties.
- **Trade policy uncertainty**: Uncertainty about trade policy could complicate buyout timing.
- **Domestic content requirements**: Buy American or similar requirements may limit sourcing options.

## Energy Code & Sustainability
- **New energy code adoption**: A new energy code taking effect during design could require changes.
- **Certification requirements**: Sustainability certification targets may add cost or documentation effort.
- **Electrification requirements**: Requirements to eliminate fossil fuel systems may affect design and utility service.
- **Material restrictions**: Embodied carbon or material content requirements could limit product choices.
- **Renewable system integration**: Solar or battery systems may require additional coordination and approvals.
